# Warm Precision

**Make the important obvious, the complex understandable, and the next step clear. Do it with warmth, restraint and respect for evidence.**

This philosophy describes an evidence-led editorial voice: scientifically credible, direct, useful, and confident without hype. It supports public curiosity and rigorous technical work through the same factual foundation. Layout changes with the task; care and clarity do not.

## 1. Meaning before styling

Finish this sentence before choosing a layout: **For this audience, this artifact should make ___ clear so they can ___.**

Choose prose for an argument, a table for attributes, a chart for quantities, and a diagram for relationships. A visual earns its place by making something easier to understand. An interface earns its structure by helping someone complete a task.

Test: can you name the reader's job without mentioning a color, font, or component?

## 2. Evidence shapes the hierarchy

For explanatory work, prefer **claim → mechanism → evidence → implication**. The mechanism may be unknown; say so instead of inventing a bridge. Distinguish observation, registered test, descriptive summary, hypothesis, illustration, and exploratory account.

The public register is curious and plain-spoken; the research register is neutral and precise. Both use the same facts. Preserve denominators, units, intervals, exclusions, and scope whenever they affect interpretation. A non-significant difference alone is not evidence of equivalence.

Test: does the visual prominence or headline imply more certainty than the evidence supports?

## 3. Attention is a limited resource

Give each composition one primary focal point, clear supporting hierarchy, and quiet context. Use proximity, whitespace, weight, and scale before color. Accent area is a ceiling, not a quota. One or two accented items can focus a figure; repeating the accent everywhere makes it meaningless.

The focal point comes from the subject: an artifact to inspect, a consequential finding, a useful question, or a plainly named tool. Ordinary pages start with descriptive headings. Display type earns its scale through the content it carries; it is not an automatic opening ceremony. A working persona can deserve a large title while its installation guide needs a modest product name.

Test: can a reader identify the primary takeaway or action in five seconds? If the opening could be pasted onto an unrelated product, replace it with something specific to this one.

## 4. Remove clutter; preserve meaning

Delete decoration, repeated labels, redundant containers, and connectors that say nothing new. Keep essential relationships, caveats, uncertainty, and information needed for the task. Necessary complexity gets an overview and a detail view.

Simplification must not silently remove a counterexample, failed condition, branch, or qualification. When adapting a complex source, record what was merged, omitted, or moved.

Test: after removing an element, can the reader still draw the same valid conclusion?

## 5. Warmth comes from care

Warmth comes from a welcoming canvas, readable typography, considerate language, thoughtful spacing, and helpful feedback. Premium character comes from coherence and precision. Purposeful imagery is welcome when it provides evidence or meaning.

Prefer flat surfaces and restrained outlines. Avoid gradients, decorative shadows, generic icon-card grids, ornamental framing, and technical-looking decoration. Controls name their action; errors name the problem and the recovery. A research conclusion can end with a next question rather than a marketing CTA.

Test: does every expressive choice help the content, reader, or task?

## 6. Meaning survives a change of scale

Semantic color roles, factual relationships, voice, and hierarchy remain stable across media. Layout, density, type size, units, and reading order adapt to the destination. Recompose rather than shrink.

A dense analytical table can be excellent Warm Precision design. A projected slide may need only one sentence and one figure. Consistency is shared logic, not identical geometry.

Test: do the phone, print, slide, and desktop versions preserve the same essential meaning?

## Modes

Choose a mode per surface, not per product:

| Mode | Reader success | Typical expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read | Understand an argument or explanation | Comfortable measure, useful headings, quiet navigation |
| Explain | Understand quantities or relationships | Direct labels, faithful encoding, overview/detail |
| Operate | Complete a task efficiently | Familiar controls, appropriate density, complete relevant states |
| Persuade | Make an informed decision and act | Claim, credible proof, deliberate pacing, one primary action |
| Experience | Engage with the work itself | Artifact-led composition; supporting interface recedes |

## Default decision sequence

1. Identify the audience, purpose, evidence, mode, and destination.
2. Choose the document type from the reader's goal ([DOCUMENTS.md](DOCUMENTS.md)) and the information structure that serves the task.
3. Apply the selected profile through the medium adapter.
4. Distill redundancy while checking evidence fidelity, and edit the copy against [VOICE.md](VOICE.md).
5. Inspect the actual output at intended sizes and conditions.
6. Publish with sources and appropriate next steps.

## Completion standard

Purpose is clear. Hierarchy matches the task or argument. Evidence is faithful. The destination is readable and usable. Nothing removable competes with essential meaning.
