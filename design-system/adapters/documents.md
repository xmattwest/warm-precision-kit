# Documents and print

## Purpose and structure

Pick the document type and its shape from [DOCUMENTS.md](../DOCUMENTS.md). Read mode leads with the argument or outcome. Use a claim, supporting explanation, evidence, qualifications, and implication. A technical paper follows its venue's section and citation conventions. A memo should not imitate a landing page.

## Type and geometry

Use the profile's document ramp: 11 pt body, 9 pt captions, 8 pt kickers, 13 pt lede, 14 pt section headings, 19 pt page heading, 24 pt display. The lede must read larger than body (13/11, not a whisper). These are print defaults, not universal minima; enlarge for the actual audience and viewing conditions. Use approximately 1.45 line height and 18 mm A4 margins. Maintain a comfortable single-column measure for memos.

Prefer typography, whitespace, and quiet rules to boxes. Use the profile's document units rather than applying digital pixels blindly. Ivory is the default digital/print canvas; required white paper, grayscale output, or house styles are scoped publishing adaptations.

## Evidence and production

Keep citations close to consequential claims. Include units, denominators, uncertainty, and limitations needed for interpretation. Avoid forcing a marketing CTA into research; end with an implication, decision, or next question.

Use semantic headings and selectable text in HTML and PDF. Browser-generated PDF is a convenient visual export, not a guarantee of a fully tagged accessible PDF; when required, use an export workflow that preserves reading order and tagging and inspect it.

Print at actual size. Check page count, widows/orphans, clipped figures, font embedding, links, and grayscale meaning. A one-page brief that does not fit should be distilled, not reduced to unreadable type.
